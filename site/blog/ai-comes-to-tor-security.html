AI Helps Secure Tor: Obsolete Relay Average Hits a Ten-Year High

Tor’s 0.4.9.13 changelog credits security fixes to the LLM report firehose.

By 1AEO Team • October 16, 2026 • Updated November 14, 2026

Securing relays

As AI helps uncover Tor security flaws, the network faces a ten-year high in its average obsolete-relay count. Our 2017–2026 reconstruction puts 2026 year-to-date (YTD) through October 16 at 22:00 Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) at 1,465 Running relays on obsolete versions—nearly three times 2025’s average of 510—and 15% of observed Running relay-hours, above the previous full-year high of 10% in 2018.

The official changelog for 0.4.9.12 and 0.4.9.13 attributes high-severity security fixes to large language model (LLM) reports. Tor announced a roughly two-week security-release cadence, subject to change; versions 0.4.9.12, 0.4.9.13 and 0.4.9.14 arrived on September 8, September 23 and October 7, 2026.

Our CollecTor reconstruction shows the obsolete relay share rising from 7% in August to 29% in September, with October month-to-date (MTD) at 24% through October 16. On September 10 (04:00 UTC), the consensus’s recommended relay versions narrowed to 0.4.9.12, reclassifying older versions and pushing the obsolete count from 3 to 6,707 in one hour.

Methods #

The reconstruction processes 84,750 complete hourly network-status consensuses from Tor’s CollecTor archive spanning January 1, 2017, at 00:00 UTC through October 16, 2026, at 22:00 UTC, counting only public relays carrying the Running flag (bridges and offline relays are excluded).

Within each hourly consensus, every Running relay’s version token after Tor on its v line is classified against that consensus’s contemporaneous server-versions line using Onionoo’s TorVersion comparator: recommended if listed, obsolete if older than every recommended version, experimental if newer than every recommended version, new_in_series if newer than all recommended versions in the same major.minor.patch series, unrecommended otherwise, and unknown when the version is missing or non-Tor or recommendations are absent. Pre-release tags such as -alpha, -rc and -dev are retained; git commit metadata is omitted.

Annual and monthly mean counts are unweighted arithmetic averages across observed consensuses in each period. Status shares divide status relay-hours by total observed Running relay-hours in the same period, which equals the ratio of those unweighted period means. Missing CollecTor consensus hours—including 461 missing hours in April 2026—are excluded rather than interpolated or treated as zero. Independent reproduction scripts and tests live in charts/ai-comes-to-tor-security/.

Keep your relay current: use Tor’s official packages and enable automatic updates on Debian or Ubuntu. Verify the running version after upgrades.
